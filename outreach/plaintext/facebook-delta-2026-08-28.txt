TWO UPDATES, one of which is a correction I owe you.


1. YOUR GOGGLES ARE PROBABLY FINE - I WAS WRONG

I said earlier that one pair of goggles cannot cover both the UV and the
fiber source, and that you need to swap eyewear when you change lens. If
that sent anyone shopping for a second pair, I apologise.

WeCreat has corrected me. The protective glasses officially supplied
with the machine are designed to protect against BOTH the 355 nm UV and
the 1064 nm IR wavelengths. If you are wearing the pair that came with
your Ultra, you do not need to swap when you change source.

Two limits, because this is eyewear and I would rather be boring than
wrong twice:

  - It applies to WECREAT'S supplied glasses. Most third-party laser
    goggles are single-band. If you are wearing anything else, check it
    is rated for the wavelength you are actually firing.

  - "Designed to protect against" is not a published optical density
    figure. I have asked WeCreat for the OD rating and the standard the
    glasses are certified to, and I will post those when I have them.

This was the one thing WeCreat flagged as incorrect out of everything I
had published. I am glad it was this one, and glad they told me.


2. THIS MAY BE A FIRMWARE REGRESSION - AND I NEED YOUR VERSION NUMBER

This is the most interesting thing anyone has found so far.

My machine reports [WeCreat Lumos :ver 000240] and its fills stall and
drift. LightBurn's development team ran the same kind of job cleanly on
an Ultra reading ver 000236 - four versions OLDER than mine. Their own
words: "looks like they have a regression."

If that holds up, this is not a design problem at all. It is something
that changed for the worse between two firmware builds - a much more
fixable kind of problem. It would also mean some Ultras in the wild are
fine and some are not, purely by which firmware they shipped with.

It is not proven. Nobody can downgrade to test it, and the two machines
were not running identical jobs. But every owner has one data point.

WHAT WOULD HELP: connect over USB and note the version string your
machine reports on connect. If you have also run a dense fill, tell me:

  - did it complete, or stall
  - what SPEED (mine fail at 12000 mm/min)
  - plain Fill or Offset Fill
  - which source - UV, fiber, MOPA, diode

Those last four matter more than they look. A slow job never gets the
chance to fail, so "works fine for me" on its own unfortunately does not
tell us anything. Chris Zambesi already ran the first one on a Flex and
it completed cleanly - which is encouraging, but he used the diode, so I
have asked him for the speed before reading too much into it.
